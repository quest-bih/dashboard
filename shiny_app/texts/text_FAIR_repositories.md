### Repositories and FAIR

<font size="3">Repositories for research data objects can be generally divided into disciplinary and general-purpose repositories. 

Our analysis found that datasets in general-purpose repositories achieve, on average, a considerably higher FAIR score than datasets in disciplinary repositories, thereby increasing data reusability on a technical level.

Although disciplinary repositories achieve a lower FAIR score on average, they have an advantage in that they provide field-specific metadata. They are often also more visible and more trustworthy to researchers in the respective field. This explains why data shared by Charité’s researchers in disciplinary repositories [is reused more](http://doi.org/10.70744/MetaROR.416.1.ea) , despite lower FAIRness of these repositories. We recommend that researchers work with disciplinary repository providers to increase the FAIRness of stored research data objects and thus increase reusability further.
