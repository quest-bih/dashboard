### FAIRness over time

<font size="3">The FAIRness (Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, Reproducibility) of research data objects can be evaluated by automatic tools that aggregate metadata embedded in the landing page or retrieved from other services (e.g. [DataCite content negotiation](https://support.datacite.org/docs/datacite-content-resolver) or [re3data](https://www.re3data.org)).

We used [F‑UJI](https://www.f-uji.net/index.php?action=home) to assess research data objects mentioned in publications by Charité authors between 2020 and 2024. F‑UJI uses 16 metrics ([specification v0.8](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15045911), software [version 4.0.0]( https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22159621)), derived from the [FAIRsFAIR project](https://www.fairsfair.eu), to assess 14 (out of 15) FAIR principles.

The heatmap illustrates how the assessments for each FAIR principle developed over time. The color of a tile is the average F‑UJI score for that principle in that year, from 0% (dark red) to 100% (dark green). Use the dropdown menu above the plot to see results for all repositories, a repository type, or an individual repository.

FAIRness increased across all four principle categories in general-purpose repositories, whereas disciplinary repositories remained stable over the 2020-2024 monitoring period. Across all repositories, the principles on persistent identifiers (F1) and on open, free and universally implementable access protocols (A1.1, A1.2) score highest and have improved the most since 2020. The principles on metadata (F2, F3) and on interoperability (I2) score lowest.
